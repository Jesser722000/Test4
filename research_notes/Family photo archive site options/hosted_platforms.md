# Hosted (no-code, no-server) platforms for a private family photo + home-video archive — state as of October 2026

*Method note for the report writer: the egress proxy blocked direct page fetches (HTTP 403 on CONNECT to support.apple.com, support.google.com, ente.com and familysearch.org, among others). Every finding below therefore comes from WebSearch result extracts and not from pages I read in full. Where an official help page is cited, the figure comes from the search engine's extract of that page. The shared web-search budget ran out before every gap could be closed, and those items are listed under Gaps. Many sources are third-party, vendor or affiliate pages. I flag these, along with anything dated before 2025.*

## 1. Chronological browsing and date editing (timeline view; editing scan dates singly, in bulk, and as approximate dates)

### Takeaway
Two services were found that store **approximate dates** natively. Flickr's "date taken" has a granularity setting (year-month, year only, or "circa"). FamilySearch Memories turns free-text dates such as "circa 1944" into "about 1944". MyHeritage's AI PhotoDater also saves its estimates as GEDCOM-style estimated dates. Google Photos, Apple Photos and Ente support only exact date/time edits. Google's bulk "set one date" works on the web only. Apple's bulk edit only shifts dates by an offset. **No documented date editor was found for Amazon Photos.** Scans that lack an EXIF date show up there as "no date", so they must be dated in the file before upload.

### Cited Findings

#### Google Photos
- On photos.google.com you can select several items, open the three-dot menu and choose "Edit date & time." You can then either **shift** all of them by the same offset, which keeps the gaps between them, or **set** one date/time for all of them — [Labnol](https://www.labnol.org/internet/change-google-photos-date-time/29008); multi-photo editing on the web has existed since May 2016 — [Android Police (2016)](https://www.androidpolice.com/2016/05/25/google-photos-web-now-lets-you-adjust-the-date-and-time-of-multiple-photos/).
- Before you save, the web dialog previews how the new times will reorder the photos — [Android Central](https://androidcentral.com/google-photos-web-now-lets-you-easily-tweak-date-and-time-multiple-photos).
- The Android and iOS apps can edit date/time one item at a time. At launch, Android needed app version 5.57 or later — [XDA Developers](https://www.xda-developers.com/edit-date-and-time-google-photos/); [PiunikaWeb (June 2022)](https://piunikaweb.com/2022/06/06/here-is-how-you-can-edit-date-and-time-in-google-photos/). How-to guides disagree on whether the Android app can bulk-edit. One guide in the result set describes a bulk option in the three-dot menu; others say bulk editing is web-only. I could not tell which guide says which — [MakeUseOf](https://www.makeuseof.com/how-to-edit-date-time-google-photos/); [Gadgets To Use (2021)](https://gadgetstouse.com/blog/2021/10/06/google-photos-change-date-time-android/). A second search turned up no evidence of batch editing on mobile — [XDA Developers](https://www.xda-developers.com/edit-date-and-time-google-photos/).
- How-to guides in the result set say an edited date is kept in Google Photos' own database and is not written into the file. A downloaded copy can therefore still show the original (scan) date. Labnol is the likely source, but the extract did not say which guide — [Labnol](https://www.labnol.org/internet/change-google-photos-date-time/29008). In Google Takeout, each photo's date, GPS, description and album membership go into a separate JSON "sidecar" file — [PixelUnion](https://pixelunion.eu/blog/how-to-keep-metadata-when-exporting-google-photos/).

#### Apple Photos / iCloud Photos
- In Photos on the Mac, choose Image > Adjust Date and Time. With several photos selected, the change is applied to all of them as an offset — [MacMost](https://macmost.com/how-to-change-photo-time-and-location-in-mac-photos.html).
- The batch tool only shifts dates; it cannot set many photos to one fixed date. A user reported that it failed when the selected photos had different dates, and the suggested fix was an AppleScript — [Apple Community thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254871096); [Apple Community script: batch change to a fixed date](https://discussions.apple.com/docs/DOC-8421). On macOS Ventura, one user found that editing the date in the Cmd-I Info panel applies it to every selected photo — [Apple Community thread](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/254421275).
- On iPhone (iOS 15 and later), you select multiple photos and use the share sheet's date/time adjustment — [9to5Mac (Nov 2021)](https://9to5mac.com/2021/11/02/change-photo-date-time-iphone-ios-15/).
- Third-party tools: osxphotos, which replaced the command-line tool photos_time_warp (archived in 2024), batch-adjusts date, time and time zone — [GitHub: photos_time_warp](https://github.com/RhetTbull/photos_time_warp). Pixata is a paid Mac app for batch date edits that keeps a change history — [Mac App Store: Pixata](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pixata/id6757112779?mt=12).

#### Amazon Photos
- Amazon's help page on editing in Amazon Photos covers crop, adjust, filter, sticker and text only. Edits are saved as a new photo and the original is left untouched — [Amazon help page (indexed copy)](https://digprjsurvey.amazon.co.uk/csad/help/node/ToexYlUPwatjXzFqqZ). No official way to edit the "date taken" was found.
- A user who scanned negatives saw "no date" in Amazon Photos even after setting dates in another app. An exiftool command fixed the date display, but it also rotated the image and stripped the ICC colour profile — [ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=66392). Photos saved by Samsung's editor also uploaded as "no date" — [Samsung Community](https://eu.community.samsung.com/t5/galaxy-s23-serie/samsung-s23-und-amazon-photos-app/td-p/10294955).
- Amazon Photos 5.0 added a "This Day" section — [Electronic Partners](https://electronic.partners/news/amazon-photos-5-0-improves-album-creation-and-editing-takes-you-into-the-past-with-new-this-day-section/). A 2026 German review describes a redesign, still rolling out, with a "Memories Carousel" and a bottom navigation bar — [Netzwelt (2026)](https://www.netzwelt.de/cloud-speicher/amazon-photos-testbericht.html).

#### Ente Photos
- A user reports that date and time are easy to edit from the standard long-press and info menus, and can be changed in bulk — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1q8z/ente_io_as_a_google_photos_alternative). Ente's FAQ describes a multi-select "Edit time" action; the list of platforms was cut off in the extract — [Ente Metadata and Editing FAQ](https://ente.com/help/photos/faq/metadata-and-editing).
- A pull request merged in March 2025 (#5131) added bulk date editing, date shifting and a new date-editing screen to the mobile app — [GitHub ente-io/ente PR #5131](https://github.com/ente-io/ente/pull/5131).

#### Flickr
- Flickr's "date taken" field has a **granularity** code: 0 = full date and time, 4 = year-month, 6 = year only, 8 = "circa". Developers are told to accept any value from 0 to 10 — [Flickr API: Photo Dates](https://www.flickr.com/services/api/misc.dates.html). A 2026 Codeberg merge request swaps the meanings of 4 and 6, which contradicts the official docs — [Codeberg fist PR #1](https://codeberg.org/VDK/fist/pulls/1).
- In 2004 a Flickr staffer confirmed that users can enter just a month and year, or just a year — [Flickr API group discussion](https://flickr.com/groups/api/discuss/99143/).
- Batch dating in Organizr: drag photos into the batch area and click "Edit dates." For a single photo, double-click it and click Dates — [Flickr Help](https://www.flickrhelp.com/hc/en-us/articles/4404064327444-Reorder-your-Flickr-Photostream).

#### FamilySearch Memories
- The Date field accepts many spellings of an approximate date and stores them in one standard form, so "abt. 1944" or "circa 1944" becomes "about 1944". Filling in the Date field lets others sort a person's Memories by date with the Filter button — [FamilySearch Community](https://community.familysearch.org/en/discussion/185006/photos-with-unknown-exact-dates).
- To add a date, click "Event," then Edit, enter the date or place, and Save — [FamilySearch Blog](https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/add-date-and-location-to-photos-stories-and-documents).
- Only the person who uploaded a memory can add its date and location — [FamilySearch Community](https://community.familysearch.org/en/discussion/139841/i-seek-to-get-my-photos-into-chronological-order).
- In the API, a memory's date and place are called its "coverage" — [FamilySearch Developers](https://developers.familysearch.org/main/docs/memories-dir).

#### MyHeritage
- **PhotoDater** launched in August 2023 and is free. It estimates the year of undated photos taken between 1860 and 1990. MyHeritage says it was within 5 years 59.2% of the time, can be off by several years, shows a confidence level and error range for each estimate, and saves estimates as GEDCOM-format estimated dates — [MyHeritage Blog (Aug 2023)](https://blog.myheritage.com/2023/08/introducing-photodater-an-exclusive-free-new-feature-to-estimate-when-old-photos-were-taken/). Family Tree Magazine reports about 85% within 10 years — [Family Tree Magazine](https://familytreemagazine.com/websites/myheritage/myheritage-photo-tools).
- Independent tests: PetaPixel got 1880 for a photo dated 1884 and 1947 for one dated 1942 — [PetaPixel (Nov 2023)](https://petapixel.com/2023/11/16/this-ai-tool-will-estimate-when-an-old-photo-was-taken/). A blogger testing known 19th-century photos found it always within 3–4 years — [Genealogy à la Carte](https://genealogyalacarte.ca/?p=41627).
- PhotoDater became available in the MyHeritage and Reimagine mobile apps in November 2023 — [MyHeritage Blog (Nov 2023)](https://blog.myheritage.com/2023/11/photodater-now-available-on-the-myheritage-and-reimagine-mobile-apps/).

### Inferences
- Every scan carries its scan date, so the most portable approach is to write a best-guess date into each file's EXIF DateTimeOriginal *before* upload. This works the same on every service (Amazon has no date editor at all) and survives export (Google keeps date edits in JSON sidecars, not in the file). On services without granularity, store approximate dates as an agreed placeholder date (for example YYYY-07-01) and put "circa YYYY" in the caption.
- Flickr is the only mainstream hosted *photo* service found with native "circa" and year-only dates. FamilySearch has approximate dates but no video and weak privacy (sections 3–4). MyHeritage's PhotoDater could help produce first-guess decades for undated prints.
- For bulk-dating a box of prints to one approximate date, the Google Photos web "set date" is the easiest mainstream option. Apple's batch tool only shifts by an offset, which is awkward when scans have differing timestamps.
- A timeline (reverse-chronological grid) is the default view in Google Photos, Apple Photos, Amazon Photos and Ente. That is general knowledge and was not re-verified here. It is only useful once the dates are corrected.

## 2. People and stories (face recognition / people tagging, captions, stories, comments or reactions, and whether relatives can contribute)

### Takeaway
Automatic face grouping exists in Google Photos, Amazon Photos, Ente (on-device) and Forever (paid add-on, photos only). On the consumer platforms these labels are **private to each account**, so a relative's identifications do not reach other viewers. Shared, named people-tagging that relatives can contribute to is strongest on the genealogy platforms: FamilySearch face tags attached to tree persons, MyHeritage and Reimagine tagging, and Ancestry's Contributor role. Story-capture services (Storyworth, Remento, StoryCorps) collect recorded or written stories but are not photo archives.

### Cited Findings

#### Google Photos
- Face groups and labels are visible only to you by default and are **not shared** when you share photos. Labels stay private to each account even when the photos are shared — [Google Photos Help: face groups](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6128838?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop).
- Manually tagging a face is not available on all accounts; accounts where face grouping was turned on after its European launch reportedly lack it — [Picasa/Google Photos resources (fan site)](https://sites.google.com/site/picasaresources/google-photos-1/edit-people-albumstags). A report said manual face tagging was coming — [PhoneArena](https://www.phonearena.com/news/google-photos-manual-face-tagging-recently-uploaded_id117264).
- Partner sharing can share the whole library or only photos of chosen face groups. Archived photos and the Locked Folder are excluded — [Metadata Fixer guide (third-party)](https://metadatafixer.com/learn/google-photos-partner-sharing-guide).
- In shared albums, a "Comments & likes" setting lets anyone add comments or likes and is on by default (older, fan-run source) — [Picasa resources: albums](https://www.sites.google.com/site/picasaresources/google-photos-1/albums-in-google-photos). Adding photos to a collaborative album requires a signed-in Google account — [Viallo (vendor blog)](https://www.viallo.app/blog/add-photos-without-account); [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/google-photos-link-sharing-permissions-settings-guide/).

#### Amazon Photos
- Amazon Photos groups photos of the same person, and users label each person by name. One source says this is on by default and can be turned off in settings — [Android Central](https://www.androidcentral.com/amazon-photos-best-photo-storage-service-youre-not-using); [Pocket-lint](https://www.pocket-lint.com/apps/news/amazon/162132-what-is-amazon-photos-and-how-do-you-use-it). An older Digital Trends article says facial recognition is Prime-only — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=1224187).
- In Family Vault, every member who can upload, including the Prime owner, chooses which photos the group sees and which stay private — [Digital Trends](https://www.digitaltrends.com/?p=1224187); [Amazon help: Amazon Photos features (indexed copy)](https://digprjsurvey.amazon.com/csad/help/node/GLSS4222BAQWWB3S).

#### Ente Photos
- Ente recognises faces and objects on the device, which powers natural-language search. Face recognition has to be switched on under Settings > Machine learning — [Ente Features](https://ente.com/features/); [It's FOSS](https://itsfoss.com/moving-from-onedrive-to-ente-photos/). A user says it is "not 100% perfect" — [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1q8z/ente_io_as_a_google_photos_alternative).
- Public album links can be view-only or can let people add photos — [Ente Help: public links](https://ente.com/help/photos/features/sharing-and-collaboration/public-links); [Tildes](https://tildes.net/~tech/1q8z/ente_io_as_a_google_photos_alternative).

#### Forever
- FOREVER Facial Recognition costs an extra fee and works on photos only, not video — [FOREVER Facial Recognition FAQ](https://support.forever.com/hc/en-us/articles/29497192175515-FOREVER-Facial-Recognition-FAQs); [FOREVER Facial Recognition](https://www.forever.com/facial-recognition).

#### FamilySearch Memories
- Clicking a photo creates a face-tag box, and existing tags appear when you hover over a face — [FamilySearch Community](https://community.familysearch.org/en/discussion/185006/photos-with-unknown-exact-dates). Each person in the tree can hold up to 1,000 memory tags — [FamilySearch Help: limits](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-many-memories-photos-stories-documents-and-audio-files-can-i-upload).
- Memories cover photos, documents, stories and audio — [FamilySearch Help: limits](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-many-memories-photos-stories-documents-and-audio-files-can-i-upload). A tag naming a living person that is *not* linked to that person in the tree is visible to everyone who sees the memory — [FamilySearch Help: privacy and visibility](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/who-can-view-the-items-uploaded-to-memories).

#### MyHeritage / Reimagine
- Relatives can be invited to add their own memories, photos and details to the tree — [MyHeritage Education](https://education.myheritage.com/?p=9356). Family sites have Site Managers and Site Members; guests cannot add content and see less — [Family Tree Magazine](https://familytreemagazine.com/websites/online-tools-sharing-family-photos-stories/).
- Reimagine (May 2023) lets you tag faces with names and link photos to a MyHeritage tree — [Business Wire press release (May 2023)](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20230530005365/en); [Genealogy Explained](https://www.genealogyexplained.com/myheritage-reimagine-app/).

#### Ancestry
- Tree sharing roles: **Guest** can view and comment. **Contributor** can view and add comments, photos and stories, but cannot edit existing content or automatically see living people. **Editor** can see living people and add, edit and remove content. Access can be changed or revoked at any time — [Ancestry Help: Sharing a Family Tree](https://help.ancestry.com/hc/en-us/articles/53954890064787-Sharing-a-Family-Tree).
- Albums organize a tree's media and can be shared with anyone who has access to the tree. You can add up to 100 items at a time — [Ancestry Help: Albums](https://help.ancestry.com/hc/en-us/articles/53933313949203-Creating-and-Managing-Albums).
- Storymaker Studio (March 2023) builds short family stories from photos and audio — [Ancestry press release via StreetInsider](https://www.streetinsider.com/Press+Releases/Ancestry%C2%AE+Launches+Storymaker+Studio/21315972.html).

#### Geni and Kindex
- Geni: only people you invite can see details of living relatives. Profiles of deceased people are public unless marked private — [Genealogy Explained: Geni review](https://www.genealogyexplained.com/geni-com-review).
- Kindex is "online family organization, record keeping, and transcription software" — [FamilySearch Blog](https://www.familysearch.org/en/blog/discovering-the-comfort-of-kindex-org). Its auto-indexing covers handwriting recognition, OCR, speech-to-text and image captioning — [RootsTech Expo Hall: Kindex](https://www.familysearch.org/fr/rootstech/expohall/kindex). Podcasters praise its side-by-side transcription editor — [FamilyLocket](https://familylocket.com/?p=50850).

#### Story-capture services
- **Storyworth**: a loved one answers weekly prompts, and a printed 6x9 book arrives at the end of the year. $99 covers up to 300 pages — [Storyworth vs Remento (vendor page)](https://welcome.storyworth.com/storyworth-vs-remento/).
- **Remento**: an 8x10 book with QR codes linking to the recordings. The base price covers up to 200 pages, and the subscription auto-renews after year one — [Remento vs Storyworth (vendor page)](https://www.remento.co/remento-vs-storyworth); [Remento journal (2026)](https://www.remento.co/journal/remento-vs-storyworth-2026-the-6-biggest-differences).
- **StoryCorps**: publishing to archive.storycorps.org sends the interview to the Library of Congress archive **regardless of privacy settings**. That collection is closed to the public at the Library — [StoryCorps Help](https://support.storycorps.org/help-center/if-i-record-an-interview-with-the-storycorps-app-or-storycorps-connect-does-that-mean-anyone-around-the-world-can-listen-to-it); [StoryCorps Help: Library of Congress](https://support.storycorps.org/help-center/are-all-interviews-in-the-online-archive-archived-at-the-library-of-congress).

#### 2025–2026 family-archive startups (self-descriptions only)
- **Heritable** (founded 2025): a private archive for photos and stories with people tagging, a family tree, shared access across generations, no ads and no AI training on memories — [Heritable](https://joinheritable.com/).
- **Nostalgia – Family Archive** (iOS): version 2.1.7 added per-photo archive notes. Free tier plus paid Plus and Pro plans — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/ng/app/nostalgia-family-archive/id6760083405).
- **Family Nest**: journals, photos, voice memos and recipes, and states it does not sell family photos for AI training — [Family Nest](https://www.familynest.io/). **Kinnect**: invite-only, with an "Echo" daily-prompt feature (May 2026 post) — [Kinnect blog](https://www.kinnect.club/blog/best-app-preserving-family-memories-stories).
- **Retro** raised $21M but is built for friends, not family archives (per a competitor's blog) — [Memory Murals](https://memorymurals.com/journal/retro-app-family-photo-sharing).

### Inferences
- If the family wants relatives to *identify people* and have those names show up for everyone, consumer face grouping (Google, Amazon, Ente) doesn't do it because labels are per-account. Shared names would need captions or descriptions, or a genealogy platform's tree-linked tags.
- A plausible hybrid: a consumer service for browsing and viewing, plus FamilySearch or Ancestry for tree-linked identifications and stories. That means keeping two copies in sync.
- Storyworth and Remento suit capturing elders' stories (prompts, voice/QR recordings) as a companion to the photo archive, not as the archive itself. StoryCorps is unsuitable for family-only material because published interviews go to the Library of Congress.
- The startups (Heritable, Nostalgia, Family Nest, Kinnect) match the brief on paper but have no track record. Longevity risk is high (section 6).

- Not found: whether Apple Photos People names sync to Shared Albums or the Shared Library, and whether Shared Album subscribers can comment or like.
- Not found: Flickr and SmugMug people tagging, comments and favourites (general knowledge says Flickr has comments and person tags; not verified).
- Not found: Ente comments or reactions on shared albums.
- No independent reviews were found for the 2025–2026 startups; all claims are their own marketing.

## 3. Video (support and length, size and quality limits)

### Takeaway
**FamilySearch Memories does not accept video.** All files are capped at 15 MB, and the suggested workaround is linking to video hosted elsewhere. No 2025–2026 change was found. Other caps: Apple Shared Albums limit videos to **15 minutes at 720p**. Flickr Pro allows **10 minutes / 1 GB**. SmugMug allows **20 minutes, 1080p, 3 GB** on every plan. FamilyAlbum allows **2 minutes free / 10 minutes Premium**. Amazon Prime includes only **5 GB of video**. Google Photos is reported at **about 10 GB per video** (secondary sources). Long home-video transfers will need SmugMug, Google, Ente or Amazon paid storage, or splitting into clips.

### Cited Findings
- **FamilySearch Memories**: photos (.jpg, .png, .tif, .bmp), documents (.pdf) and audio (.mp3, .m4a, .wav) up to 15 MB each. There is no overall upload cap, but each person record can hold at most 1,000 memory tags — [FamilySearch Help: limits](https://www.familysearch.org/en/help/helpcenter/article/how-many-memories-photos-stories-documents-and-audio-files-can-i-upload). The developer guide says video files are not yet supported — [FamilySearch Developers: Memories integration](https://www.familysearch.org/developers/docs/guides/integration-memories); see also [FamilySearch Developers: Memories certification](https://www.familysearch.org/developers/docs/certification/memories). A 2023 moderator answer confirms video uploads aren't allowed and suggests linking to video hosted elsewhere and splitting oversized files — [FamilySearch Community (2023)](https://community.familysearch.org/en/discussion/comment/493293). Coverage of RootsTech 2026 (March 5–7, 2026) turned up no announcement of video in Memories. The Family Tree Magazine recap excerpt cut off before any Memories details, so this is absence of evidence only — [Family Tree Magazine RootsTech 2026 recap](https://familytreemagazine.com/blog/news/rootstech-2026-recap-genealogy-news-and-announcements/).
- **Apple iCloud Shared Albums**: videos up to 15 minutes, delivered at up to 720p. Photos are reduced to 2048 px on the long edge (panoramas up to 5400 px wide). Limits are 100 subscribers and 5,000 photos and videos per album. Originals in albums you own stay full-resolution in iCloud and count toward your storage. Limits depend on the software version the album was created with — [Apple Support: Shared Albums limits](https://support.apple.com/en-gb/148868). An April 2026 forum thread reports videos being heavily downscaled and originals not being retrievable from shared albums — [Apple Community thread (Apr 2026)](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256274936).
- **Google Photos**: most secondary sources give a 10 GB maximum per video. One article says Google's consumer help page lists 10 GB — [Cloudspress](https://www.cloudspress.com/?p=618223); German blogs report the same cap — [GoogleWatchBlog (2019)](https://www.googlewatchblog.de/2019/08/google-fotos-uploads-videos/); [GoogleWatchBlog (2020)](https://www.googlewatchblog.de/2020/03/google-fotos-alben-qualitaetsstufen/). A beginner guide's "15 GB" figure looks unreliable — [Level1Techs WarpSpeed](https://warpspeed.level1techs.com/?p=12157). ## 4. Privacy and sharing (invite-only vs. link, accounts needed, member counts, ease for elderly relatives)

### Takeaway
For non-technical relatives, the easiest viewing options need **no account**: a Google Photos album link, an Apple Shared Album "public website", an Ente public link (with optional password and expiry), a SmugMug unlisted + password gallery, or a Flickr guest pass. These are effectively "anyone with the link" rather than invite-only. True invite-only sharing needs accounts: Apple Shared Library (6 people), Amazon Family Vault (owner + 5), Ancestry (viewers need Ancestry accounts), MyHeritage family sites and FamilyAlbum. **FamilySearch Memories is not family-only by default**: memories of deceased people are public and can show up in search engines.

### Cited Findings

#### Google Photos
- Anyone with an album link can view without a Google account. Adding photos needs a signed-in account even when collaboration is on — [Guiding Tech](https://www.guidingtech.com/google-photos-link-sharing-permissions-settings-guide/); [Viallo (vendor)](https://www.viallo.app/blog/add-photos-without-account). Album links cannot have a password or expiry — [Viallo (vendor)](https://www.viallo.app/blog/share-google-photos-album).
- Google's help page states a 20,000-photo limit on *conversations* and mentions automatically sharing your entire library with someone (partner sharing) — [Google Photos Help: Share photos and videos](https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6131416?hl=en_GB). 2018 reports put shared albums at 20,000 items — [Android Police (2018)](https://www.androidpolice.com/2018/12/19/google-photos-bumps-album-limit-up-to-20000-pictures/); [PhoneArena (2018)](https://www.phonearena.com/news/Google-doubles-cap-on-photo-albums-to-20000-images_id112113). - **Permanent.org**: all uploads are private by default, and you share selected files with chosen people — [Permanent.org](https://permanent.org/?p=3689).

### Inferences
- **Elderly relatives:** the lowest-friction pattern is a single link that opens in a browser or TV with no login (Google album link, Apple public website, Ente public link with password, SmugMug password gallery). The trade-off is that anyone holding the link can view, and only Ente and SmugMug add a password. Invite-only systems (Apple Shared Library, Amazon Family Vault, Ancestry, MyHeritage) mean each elder has to create and remember an account.
- "Family-only privacy" is best met by Ente (end-to-end encrypted, password-protected links), SmugMug (password galleries) or Flickr (Family privacy level plus guest passes). Google and Apple public links are unlisted rather than protected.
- FamilySearch should be treated as a **public** genealogy layer, not a private archive. Its own docs contradict each other on defaults, and memories of living people may be searchable.

- No usability study or review was found specifically on elderly or non-technical relatives using these services. Ease-of-use claims above are inferred.

- Not found: whether viewing public FamilySearch memories requires signing in.
- Not found: Forever, Kindex and Tinybeans sharing models and member limits.

## 6. Data portability and longevity (export, metadata on export, company stability, 2023–2026 shutdowns, removals and policy changes)

### Takeaway
On export, Google Takeout moves dates, descriptions and GPS into **separate JSON sidecars**, not into the files. Edited dates and captions are lost unless merged back with ExifTool or a similar tool, and MP4 video is the hardest to fix. Ente's export also writes **JSON sidecars** (Google-Takeout-compatible) next to unchanged originals. It adds a **"Legacy" account-inheritance feature**. Changes in 2023–2026 include: Google ended Drive-for-desktop photo backup on August 10, 2026, and quietly removed the "Me Meme" feature; Amazon shut Amazon Drive and ended PhotosPlus in September 2024; and SmugMug retired its Basic/Power plans. Smaller and newer players (Forever, Kindex, the 2025 startups) carry more longevity risk. Permanent.org and Forever explicitly pitch endowment or guarantee-fund models.

### Cited Findings

#### Google Photos
- Takeout puts each photo's date, GPS, description and album membership in a JSON sidecar. Software that ignores sidecars shows no date, and photos can appear with the download day as their date and without GPS — [PixelUnion](https://pixelunion.eu/blog/how-to-keep-metadata-when-exporting-google-photos/). One ExifTool user argues the embedded EXIF survives and only file-system timestamps are lost — [ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=79605).
- Google changed Takeout's sidecar file names to include "supplemental-metadata", which broke existing ExifTool scripts — [ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=86458). ExifTool (and GUI tools such as Google Photos Takeout Helper and Metadata Fixer) can merge JSON back into the files. One user reports this works for everything except MP4. Comments and sharing data may not come back — [ExifTool forum](https://exiftool.org/forum/index.php?msg=80113); [PhotoStructure forum](https://forum.photostructure.com/t/how-do-you-manage-google-takeouts/757); [ComputerBase forum](https://www.computerbase.de/forum/threads/google-fotos-uebertragen-schlaegt-fehl.2276038/).
